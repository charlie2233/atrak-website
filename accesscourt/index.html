Every athlete deserves a way into the game.

AccessCourt expands access to movement, confidence, and community through supervised adaptive basketball and accessible coaching technology.

Partner with us Explore the pilot

AccessCourt is a student-led Atrak Impact initiative in pilot development in Irvine, California. It is not yet an independent 501(c)(3), is not soliciting donations, and does not represent that contributions are tax-deductible.

Access begins with listening.

We bring student athletes, engineers, educators, families, and host organizations together to reduce barriers to participation. The work is educational and recreational—not therapy, diagnosis, or medical care.

Build with the community, test in supervised settings, and improve only from real feedback.

Three connected programs

The pilot starts small: one cohort, one host organization, and tools that work without personal accounts or participant data.

01

Adaptive Clinics

Supervised, station-based basketball sessions with seated and standing options, visual schedules, simpler instructions, sensory breaks, and no elimination or ranking.

02

Visual Drill Coach

A no-login, multilingual coaching tool that presents one instruction at a time with large visuals, speech support, and adjustable complexity.

Try the working MVP →
03

Student Builder Corps

Student peer coaches and engineers train under adult supervision, solve partner-defined problems, and document improvements without accessing sensitive records.

How the pilot works

Each cycle stays accountable to the people who will use it.

  1. 1

    Listen

    Interview educators, participants, and families about real barriers and useful support.

  2. 2

    Design

    Create accessible drills, visuals, communication supports, and a documented safety plan.

  3. 3

    Pilot

    Run a small, invitation-only clinic with trained adults and host staff present.

  4. 4

    Improve

    Review incidents, near misses, accessibility feedback, and aggregate outcomes before repeating.

Safety and privacy are launch requirements.

A clinic does not run unless adult supervision, partner staff, insurance confirmation, consent, emergency planning, and required training are in place.

Adult-led safeguarding

Screened and trained adults hold legal and safety responsibility. Student volunteers never supervise participants alone.

No recording by default

The first pilots use a no-participant-camera rule. Optional future media consent is separate from permission to participate.

Minimum necessary data

We do not request diagnoses, IEP files, face data, or participant accounts. Host staff retain need-to-know support information.

No medical claims

We measure participation, clarity, return interest, accommodations, and safety—not treatment outcomes or disability change.

Operational policies are being prepared for adult review and must be approved by the legal operating partner, insurer, and host organization before the first clinic.

A clear partnership model

AccessCourt builds the program with—not around—the host organization.

AccessCourt prepares

  • Adaptive drill plan and visual materials
  • Student volunteer recruitment and training
  • Visual Drill Coach and printed alternatives
  • Equipment list, run-of-show, and debrief
  • Aggregate pilot evaluation and improvements

The host organization provides

  • Qualified staff who know the participants
  • Participant consent and emergency contacts
  • Medication, personal care, and behavior support
  • Accessible facility, emergency plan, and AED access
  • Insurance and safeguarding coordination

Help shape the first pilot.
